# Waystone — narrative design

**Status:** designed, unimplemented, and not validated in play. Organized 2026-10-19 from the narrative proposal previously held in Design Foundations. The story and fragment powers have not received a new design review as part of this documentation change. [Development scope](development-scope.md) records the outstanding questions; [Current state](current-state.md) owns implementation status.

## The broken Waystone

The fiction gives the game's time and memory mechanics a place in its world.

Someone trying to keep themselves or a loved one alive broke the Waystone. The original stone anchored time into one stable history. Its breaking scattered fragments among several people. Ordinary creatures freeze completely; the player's small fragment lets them remain conscious in stopped time and retain memory through resets.

The reason the player carries a fragment remains open, pending the Campaign's characters and ending.

## The vendor and the final bearer

The person who broke the Waystone is the proposed final boss and a vendor present throughout the game. They are the only NPC who subtly moves while time is stopped.

The tell is mechanically real and becomes more apparent over time: eyes following the player, a shifted hand, a caught movement, and eventually an open display of their power.

## Fragment authority

Fragment authority has several levels. Major bosses can use **Counterstop**: after the player commits to an attack and that attack becomes readable, the bearer freezes everyone, including itself, chooses a reaction, and resumes alongside the committed attack. This gives the bearer a decision after receiving committed information.

The final bearer can briefly stop the player's body and take one bounded stolen action. Control returns to the player with unlimited thinking time before a damaging threat becomes active. The player's collected fragments increasingly contest that authority, approaching parity by the finale.

The intended experience preserves the game's safe thinking state throughout these powers. Their timing, presentation, fairness, and narrative usefulness still require prototyping.
